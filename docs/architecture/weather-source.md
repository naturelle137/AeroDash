# METAR source

Flight Prep retrieves an observation on request from [wxdecoder](https://wxdecoder.com/api/). Its free, keyless API permits reasonable non-commercial education and situational awareness, requires attribution and cache compliance, and prohibits use as an official briefing or go/no-go authority. Commercial use requires separate permission. On 21 November 2026 `/api/wx/EDDF` returned HTTP 200, `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`, a raw report and an observation `reportTime` distinct from retrieval time. Coverage depends on its upstream feeds; absence is shown explicitly. The browser honours the response cache headers. The CSP permits this one origin; no credentials are sent.

The station, raw report and UTC observation time must agree. Unknown, malformed or future timestamps are rejected; observations over one hour old are marked outdated. This threshold is an application warning, not an aviation validity guarantee. Offline, empty and failed requests are distinct from successful observations. A fresh observation with liquid precipitation can default an empty runway condition for the matching ICAO station; the pilot can override it. The suggestion expires with the observation and must be saved explicitly. Saved and manual conditions take precedence. Saved weather-derived conditions retain their station and observation time; the pilot must verify current conditions. Present-weather groups are interpreted using the [Aviation Weather Center METAR format](https://aviationweather.gov/help/data/); vicinity, recent weather, trend forecasts and remarks do not indicate precipitation at the station. Unsupported or malformed report groups do not produce a suggestion. Wet / soft ground is the available unpaved category, not a determination that ground is soft.

A pilot may explicitly replace wind, temperature or QNH individually from a current report. Unsupported, missing or ambiguous values are not offered; wind with a variable direction or reported directional range is not offered. Applied values show station, source and observation time until edited. Wind direction is true; gusts are not applied. An A-group altimeter setting is converted from inHg to hPa using the [NIST conversion factor](https://www.nist.gov/pml/owm/metric-si/unit-conversion/pressure-and-gas-flow-unit-conversions) and rounded to 0.01 hPa. The QNH pressure-altitude method remains a separate pilot choice. Obtain required weather from an authorised briefing source.

The [NOAA Aviation Weather Center API](https://aviationweather.gov/data/api/) documents worldwide METAR access without a key, but a request with an Origin header returned no `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` header; a browser cannot call it directly. [Datamask](https://www.datamask.org/api-docs) excludes flight planning.

## TAF runway suggestion

Flight Prep embeds the existing TAF retrieval panel. The raw station, issue time and validity must match response metadata. Forecast validity is bounded to 48 hours and issuance must be no earlier than 24 hours before validity starts (application rejection limits, not an aviation validity guarantee). The current UTC time, not a planned departure/arrival time, selects applicable weather within the half-open validity interval. Parsing follows the [FAA TAF format](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/publications/aim_html/chap7_section_1.html): initial and FM groups require wind, visibility and sky; FM replaces prevailing weather. BECMG inherits omitted weather after its end and suppresses suggestions during its uncertain transition. Applicable TEMPO and PROB30/40 periods can suggest Wet for possible liquid precipitation without asserting observed rain. Their end is exclusive. Unknown, malformed or unsupported groups suppress the entire TAF suggestion; no weather is inferred from raw substrings. This bounded parser does not support temperature extrema, wind shear or remarks.

A matching fresh liquid METAR takes precedence over a matching liquid TAF. Forecast changes, failed refresh, station edits, expiry, offline events and panel removal withdraw unsaved TAF suggestions. Saved and pilot-entered conditions retain priority. Saved TAF provenance includes station, issue time and the full forecast validity interval; it is a historical pilot declaration, never a live runway assessment. Legacy unpaved Wet / soft ground declarations remain readable.

## Forecast wind and observation fallback

Flight Prep offers the prevailing TAF wind for the selected flight time and matching station when that time lies in the forecast validity, using the same bounded parser and station, issue-time and validity checks. Only numeric directions in knots or m/s are offered; variable direction, km/h, gusts, TEMPO/PROB groups, BECMG transitions and unsupported groups are not. The TAF supplies neither temperature nor QNH here. For each parameter without an applicable forecast, an unexpired METAR value is shown as a METAR observation without a forecast, with its station and observation time. Both sources show their station and elapsed time; a station mismatch is flagged. Nothing is replaced until the pilot chooses it, and manual input and offline use are unchanged. Applied provenance names the TAF or METAR, issue or observation time and validity. DWD ICON remains a separate pilot-selected forecast below.

## Hourly forecast

Pilot-entered temperature, aviation QNH and wind can be applied separately offline for a selected phase, location and future device-local time. The entries are not an official forecast or saved; changing the selection clears them. Missing or invalid fields and a passed selected time are shown, and expired entries cannot be applied. Explicitly applied values retain pilot-entered provenance until edited.

The pilot can copy a UTC flight time into the local selection. A UTC instant mapping to the second occurrence of a repeated local time is rejected because the local input cannot represent it; the first occurrence still requires confirmation. Changing the selected time clears forecast entries.

[DWD WAWFOR](https://www.dwd.de/DE/leistungen/lf_20_wawfor/wawfor_techndoc_3_7.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=2) lists hourly QNH in hPa for global and European grids, but its data endpoint returned HTTP 401 on 22 November 2026; free browser access and eligible terms are not established. Forecast QNH is unavailable from the integrated services. The pilot can enter aviation QNH from another source in hPa or inHg and optionally record that source and its local issue or retrieval time. The optional time type distinguishes issue from retrieval and is retained with applied provenance; an unspecified type is flagged. A source time in the future blocks QNH application; the app does not assess its freshness. inHg is converted using the NIST factor above and rounded to 0.01 hPa. Unit-specific drafts prevent reinterpretation while preserving entries on switching; their presence is visible and the selected draft can be cleared independently; clearing or changing the selection removes both. The source is a pilot declaration, not verified by the app. Missing source, source time and validity are flagged; a declared source time shows elapsed minutes or days without asserting freshness.

Manual QNH accepts finite decimal or scientific notation, consistent with pressure-altitude input; hexadecimal and binary notation are rejected. Valid manual QNH entries show the hPa value that will replace the current Flight Prep QNH before application; invalid entries show a correction prompt. The selected phase, location, device-local time and its UTC interpretation are shown beside the entry for comparison with source validity; the pilot must check the interpretation around clock changes. Applied provenance retains the original entered QNH and unit alongside the converted value. A clear action removes the QNH entry and its source without changing an already applied value. Application remains explicit and works offline.

The manual QNH entry also displays the current Flight Prep QNH and its unit, or indicates that no value is entered, before replacement.

The pilot may declare a QNH validity interval in device-local time. Both endpoints are required when either is entered; the selected flight time must lie in the interval, including its start and excluding its end. The interpreted UTC source time and validity endpoints are shown and retained with the applied value; the declaration is not verified against the source. Clearing the entry or changing the selection clears the interval.

Validity feedback distinguishes missing endpoints, nonexistent local times, reversed intervals and flight times outside the interval.

Nonexistent local times during a clock change are rejected for the selected flight time and the QNH source and validity times. A repeated flight time requires confirmation of the browser's displayed UTC interpretation before retrieval or application of any forecast value. Repeated local QNH source or validity times separately require confirmation before QNH application. Changing the relevant times clears confirmation. Applied provenance includes the selected flight time in UTC; the other occurrence cannot be selected through the local-time input.

[wxdecoder](https://wxdecoder.com/api/) provides free, keyless hourly NWS forecasts for US airports under the attribution and non-commercial terms above. On 21 November 2026 KDFW and KJFK returned CORS-enabled responses; EDDF returned no hourly coverage. The [NWS API](https://www.weather.gov/documentation/services-web-api) describes hourly periods. The selection matches only the exact timestamp provided, interpreted from device local time, with no interpolation. Temperature is converted from °F to °C; compass direction and knots are displayed as supplied. The source's forecast fetch timestamp is retained with applied values and is not an issue time. Source stale flags or fetch age over four hours suppress application; this threshold is an application warning. Browser caching follows the source headers. Forecast QNH is unavailable; sea-level pressure is never substituted.

Grass is explicitly distinguished from other or legacy unpaved surfaces. A valid matching METAR with heavy rain, or a currently applicable TAF precipitation period longer than two hours, suggests soft ground for grass only. A TAF prevailing period is bounded by a later prevailing change; uncertain transitions suppress inference. Temporary and probability periods do not establish continuous precipitation duration. Other liquid precipitation suggests wet. These are unverified weather-based suggestions, not runway observations; pilot declarations take precedence. Existing unpaved records retain their original meaning.

US and global forecast requests run independently; changing the shared flight selection or going offline invalidates both, and changing coordinates invalidates only the global request.

A forecast request that cannot be sent names its specific reason (offline, missing or invalid location, coordinates or time, passed time, a time not on an exact hour or UTC hour) instead of a generic prompt; no request is sent in these cases.

Responses with duplicate forecast instants are rejected rather than selecting an ambiguous value. Invalid global calendar dates are rejected instead of being normalized by the browser.

## Global model forecast

On explicit request, Flight Prep sends pilot-entered coordinates to the [Open-Meteo forecast API](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs). Its [free terms](https://open-meteo.com/en/terms) permit non-commercial use and require [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) attribution. Coverage is worldwide, subject to model availability. On 22 November 2026 an HTTPS request returned CORS `*`. The API provides instantaneous 2 m dew point in °C for the selected hour, displayed as model data without applying it to calculations. The API also provides the maximum 10 m gust over the preceding hour in knots; it is displayed but not applied to calculations. The API provides hourly instantaneous 2 m temperature (°C) and 10 m wind (knots, true degrees); the app requests UTC and matches the selected exact hour without interpolation. The returned model grid coordinates are validated, displayed beside the requested coordinates and retained with applied values; they can differ from the requested location. The model may use interpolated grid data and is not an aviation briefing. The API does not provide model issue time, so retrieval time is shown separately and does not establish forecast freshness. After four hours or the selected time the application withdraws the values. Missing fields remain unavailable. The temperature–dew point spread is the arithmetic difference of the two displayed model values, rounded to 0.1 °C; it is unavailable if either is missing or the dew point exceeds the temperature, is not a cloud-base or fog forecast and is not applied. Model mean sea-level pressure (`pressure_msl`, checked 26 November 2026: unit `hPa`) is shown as model data labelled not aviation QNH; it is never applied or substituted for QNH. A missing or non-hPa unit makes only that value unavailable. Coordinates and the user's IP address reach the provider.

Australian [Bureau of Meteorology Area QNH](https://www.bom.gov.au/aviation/forecasts/area-qnh/) is published for defined zones in hPa with three-hour UTC validity ([product description](https://www.bom.gov.au/aviation/data/education/area-qnh.pdf)). On 23 November 2026 its page directed flight planning users to Airservices Australia; a browser-origin HTTP request returned 403 on 23 November 2026, and reuse terms and reliable location-to-zone mapping were not established. It is not integrated as an automatic source.

Open-Meteo describes ICON Global, Europe and Central Europe coverage; coverage and forecast horizon depend on the model. On 24 November 2026 a cross-origin HTTPS request for Frankfurt returned HTTP 200 and CORS `*`. This model is not aviation QNH or an official briefing. The separate global forecast remains available.
